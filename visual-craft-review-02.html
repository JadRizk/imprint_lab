00 — VerdictWhat it adds up to

At the level of vocabulary the thesis is delivered: zero radius everywhere, no gradient, no pastel, no soft shadow, mono body, one accent. At the level of structure it is not. Two failures dominate, and they compound.

First, the value ramp does not render. --color-surface sits 1.03:1 against the ground — a difference no display and no eye resolves — yet 34 elements on /components participate in it. Panels do not sink and do not layer; they are simply not there. Second, the accent has stopped being a signal and become the texture: 18.7 lime events per screen on the reference page, where lime is the second most common text colour, outranking both white and body-tertiary.

Underneath both sits the same omission. This is a system whose entire visual language is line, and it ships one line weight, two font weights, and a glow token that has never rendered in a browser. The three strongest levers a line-and-light system has on near-black are all untouched.

What was run: apps/docs on :3001 — /, /systems/human-laboratory, /components, /example, /report, plus static/catalog.html and static/example-report.html. Captures are 1:1 headless Chrome. The 375 px headless captures proved unreliable (Chrome clamped the layout viewport and cropped), so every narrow-width claim here was re-measured in a live browser instead — the one such claim, the nav collision in §07, is a measured 6 px, not an artifact.

01 — The squint testWhat survives when you stop reading

Four full pages at the same scale. Blur them and only the elements with real value separation persist — everything carried by a 1 px #333 hairline dissolves at the first step.

The last panel is the argument for the whole review. The report kit spends lime twice in 5,200 px and is the most legible thing in the repo. The app pages spend it seventy times and are the least.

The two hairline tokens are 1.11:1 apart — and they are the wrong way round

--color-ambient #3A3A3A is lighter than --color-line #333333. The token documented as “decoration only… grid overlays, rules, idle brackets” (tokens/theme.css:27-29) is the more visible of the pair. The consequence is visible inside ImageFrame: the 40 px grid overlay drawn in ambient (ui/components/image-frame.module.css:8-9) is brighter than the frame border drawn in line (ui/components/image-frame.tsx:52). The decoration outranks the structure that contains it.

The same inversion runs through BentoCard: its idle corner brackets are border-ambient and its own edge is border-line (ui/components/bento-card.tsx:68, 82). At 1.11:1 apart they read as one continuous hairline, so the bracket motif — the thing that is supposed to say this is a bounded instrument — never separates from the box it is bracketing.

Does a line-based system need a value ramp at all?

No — and the system already says so. BRAND.md: “Borders are the structure. Not shadows, not elevation, not spacing alone.” Elevation is the wrong metaphor here and the ramp is a vestige of reaching for it.

The honest move is to stop tuning the greys and spend the budget on line instead. A surface token you cannot see is not subtle; it is a distinction that exists only in the stylesheet, and it invites more of the same — bg-surface already appears on 16 elements of /components and bg-canvas on 18, thirty-four participants in a difference nobody can perceive. What the system is missing is not a third grey. It is a second line weight. That is §04.

Grepping the CSS the dev server actually serves for the accent value or its role alias returns 28 distinct rules: 18 static, 9 hover, 1 focus. They resolve to seven separate jobs.

Keep lime for

The brand mark. The focus ring. The active nav item. Status. And one emphasis per view — the thing the view exists to show.

Severity is inverted in the status pair

--color-warning #FF8A00 measures 8.11:1 against the ground. --color-critical #FF4A4A measures 5.78:1. On near-black, luminance is urgency — so warning currently shouts 1.4× louder than critical. Constraint 6 is right and is not the problem: amber does read as the accent on this ground, and orange is the correct family. The fix is lightness, not hue.

04 — Line weightOne hairline doing every job

Two weights exist, and the 2 px is almost entirely one motif — the 12 px corner bracket, plus a single border-l-2 quote rule at apps/docs/app/systems/human-laboratory/example/page.tsx:99. Functionally the system has one structural weight. A card edge, a table row divider, a section rule, a panel boundary, the page header's underline, an input, the nav's baseline — all 1 px #333, all 1.52:1. Every boundary asserts itself exactly as hard as every other boundary, which is why the squint test in §01 loses all of them at once.

This is the largest unexploited lever in the system, because line is the whole vocabulary. A rule ladder is how print builds hierarchy without colour, elevation or radius — and it maps directly onto roles the system already has names for.

The cost is one new token and a re-point of --color-ambient, which also corrects the inversion in §02. No new colour semantics, no elevation, no radius — the ladder lives entirely inside the existing constraints.

The bracket doesn't scale with what it brackets

The corner marker is fixed at 12 px / 2 px regardless of its container — size-3 in ui/components/bento-card.tsx:82, calc(var(--spacing) * 3) in ui/components/image-frame.module.css:51-56. On the hero's 590 px frame it is a speck at 2% of the edge; on a 102 px card it is proportionate at 12%. A motif that means this is a bounded instrument should hold constant as a ratio, not as a pixel count. Three steps would cover the whole system: 12 px below 200 px, 20 px to 480 px, 28 px above.

The fourth cell is the shape emission actually has: a bright, small-radius core with a long low-alpha falloff and no offset. The shipped token is all falloff and no core, which is why it reads as haze rather than light.

The one place the system takes bloom seriously is the scan-line utility (tokens/theme.css:140-144) — four stacked shadows out to 80 px plus a 40 px gradient trail. It is the best-looking thing in the codebase and it appears once per page, inside a 160 px specimen box.

Is emissive light a different thing from a drop shadow?

Yes — and the distinction is precisely the one the thesis is already drawing.

A drop shadow is a lie about the third dimension. It asserts that a flat rectangle floats above another flat rectangle, lit by an imaginary sun at a conventional angle. It is skeuomorphic, it is soft, it has an offset, and it is exactly the vocabulary constraint 3 exists to reject.

Bloom makes no claim about depth. It is a claim about energy. A phosphor that is emitting spills into its neighbours because that is what light physically does on a CRT — the shader literature separates diffusion (light scattering as it passes through the glass) from halation (a bright region illuminating adjacent dark areas by internal reflection), and both scale with emitted energy, not with geometry. Rendering that is not softening the interface. It is saying this element is on.

Which gives the rule: glow follows current, not geometry. It has no offset, a tight bright core, and a radius proportional to how live the thing is. On a near-black ground it is also the single strongest lever available, because a 16.83:1 accent with a halo reads as luminous rather than merely coloured — and nothing else in the system can produce that quality.

Where glow belongs, and where it cheapens
BelongsWhy
The focus ringCurrently a flat 2px outline. A focus ring that emits is unmistakable, costs no new colour, and finally differentiates focus from the eight hover rules that share its hue.
The active nav itemThe one item that is currently true. Today it is the same lime as a hover, so navigation state is indistinguishable from pointer state.
A live or just-changed readoutRENDERING, a streaming value, a number that ticked. This is what the -lg token should have been for.
The growing edge of a revealImageFrame's scan line — the one place the system gets this exactly right (ui/components/image-frame.module.css:38-46).
The one highlighted mark in a chartThe report kit's target line already does this.

Where it cheapens

Ambient use. A glow on every card, a glow on every button, and above all glow on hover — group-hover:shadow-lime-glow on the colour swatches (…/components/page.tsx:69). Hover is not a state of the system; it is a state of the pointer. Emission should mean the machine is doing something, not that a mouse is nearby.

Either give --shadow-lime-glow-lg the focus-ring and live-readout jobs, or delete it. Shipping a token nothing has ever rendered is worse than not having one.

06 — TypographyTwo weights out of five, and a heading level that isn't

The weight ladder is binary

Both families load 300/400/500/600/700 — ten font files (apps/docs/app/layout.tsx:5-15). Measured across every element on both dense routes, the rendered weights are 400 and 700. Nothing else, anywhere.

Six faces ship and never render, across both families. The binary 400/700 is why the interface reads flat even where it is dense: there is regular, and there is shouting.
  • 500 is the instrument-label weight. text-micro at 400 with 0.2em tracking is frail at 10 px on a dark ground — light-on-dark optically thins strokes, which is why terminal faces historically shipped heavier. A 10 px label at 500 holds its colour without becoming a heading. This single change fixes every eyebrow in the system.
  • 700 is doing two incompatible jobs — headings and inline emphasis in body copy (raw structure, a machine for communicating information on the thesis page). At 16 px mono, inline 700 is a hard stop that breaks the line. Give inline emphasis 600 and reserve 700 for headings.
  • 300 is a display-only option. At 60 px, Space Grotesk 300 on obsidian is genuinely elegant and instrument-like. At 16 px it disintegrates. If it is not going to be used at ≥48 px, drop it from the loader.

The scale is under-occupied in the middle

Sizes rendered on /example at 1440: 10, 12, 14, 16, 24, 60 px — six of eleven steps, with nothing between 24 and 60. That 2.5× leap is where a subhead lives. On /components, 12 px carries 230 of 333 text nodes and 10 px carries 70 — 90% of all text on the reference page is ≤12 px, and 12 px alone is 69% of it. The system is far denser than it is hierarchical. The closed scale is not the problem; the problem is that most of its steps are decorative, existing only to be listed on the page that lists them.

The pairing is right. Defend it.

Space Grotesk for headings against IBM Plex Mono for body is the strongest single decision in the system. It is what makes this read as an instrument rather than a brutalist poster, and the two faces are close enough in skeleton to feel like one voice and far enough apart to signal role. Do not touch it.

What is wrong is the measure. The thesis page uses PageShell width="prose" = max-w-3xl = 768 px (ui/components/page-shell.tsx:12), which at 16 px IBM Plex Mono is ~80 characters. Eighty columns is a terminal convention, not a reading measure — monospace wants 60–72 because every character is the width of an m. prose should be roughly max-w-[52ch].

The defect that matters most

apps/docs/app/systems/human-laboratory/page.tsx:36 applies className="thesis". No .thesis rule exists anywhere in the repo — it is a hook someone intended to fill and never did. With Tailwind's preflight unstyling headings, every <h2> in the rendered BRAND.md computes to:

font-size: 16px · font-weight: 400 · color: #A3A3A3 · margin-top: 0

— identical to the paragraph above it in every property except the family, and at 16 px/400 that family difference is nearly invisible. Four section headings — Thesis, Voice, Typography, Non-negotiables — are typographically indistinguishable from body copy, on the page whose entire job is to state the system's position on hierarchy. Squint at it in §01: one grey rectangle, 1,400 px tall.

A brutalist nav is opaque, or it is a rule and nothing else.

02 · A 6px collision at 375

The same nav is fixed, and content clears it with a hard-coded pt-12 whose comment reads “pt-12 clears the fixed nav” (…/example/page.tsx:37-39). At 375 px the nav wraps to three lines and measures 102 px, so its bottom rule cuts 6 px into the RESEARCH_OBJECTIVE eyebrow. At 768 px the nav is 54 px and there is 42 px of clearance. A fixed element whose height is variable cannot be cleared by a constant.

03 · The empty swatch

systems/human-laboratory/static/catalog.html — the SUCCESS slot renders an empty bordered box with “+ accent” as its value. The intent (success is the accent) is correct; the rendering reads as a missing asset. Draw the accent chip and label it, or state it in words and drop the box.

04 · The black fifth series

The catalog's bar chart renders an “Other” bar at #000000 on #0F0F0F — a hole rather than a category. The fold-in slot needs a value: --color-line, or a textured neutral using the .texture mask the chart layer already ships (static/thl.chart.css:167-169).

05 · The card that breathes

BentoCard combines overflow-hidden with whileHover: {scale: 1.01} (ui/components/bento-card.tsx:52, 68). A 1% scale on a hard-bordered rectangle lands the border on fractional pixels and produces a shimmer at the moment of hover. A brutalist card should snap — scale by a whole pixel, or don't scale.

08 — The chart paletteAn objection I tested and withdrew

The obvious criticism of the report kit is that its four series — green #11813C, blue #2098DB, purple #7258B4, pink #CC689E — look like a generic analytics dashboard dropped into a brutalist page. They span nearly the whole hue wheel at high chroma and carry none of the system's identity. Standing in front of catalog.html, that is exactly how it reads.

So I tried to fix it, using the repo's own validator.

Every attempt to make the palette feel more like the system fails its own floors. The wide hue spread is forced, not chosen.

Four hue-identified categorical series on near-black, with all-pairs CVD separation, is a demanding enough problem that the solution space is nearly a point. The palette is close to the only answer, and the reasoning in static/thl.chart.css:6-35 — accent excluded at OKLCH L 0.944, status hues reserved, four as the ceiling — is correct on every count I could check.

Which relocates the recommendation. The chart palette is not a colour problem to solve; it is a form problem to avoid. If four categorical hues always look foreign here, then the fix is to need them less often: prefer small multiples over stacked series, lean on the .texture mask and on value where identity permits, and reserve the accent for the single mark the chart exists to make. The report kit's own latency chart already demonstrates this — one blue line, one lime target, one lime endpoint — and it is the most on-brand chart in the repo precisely because it uses one series.

09 — ReferencesWhere to look next

Grouped by the lever each one informs. Each entry says what to take and, more usefully, what not to.

Line weight · label weight · register

U.S. Graphics Company — TX-02 Berkeley Mono

usgraphics.com/products/berkeley-mono · and the TX-02 datasheet (PDF)

The closest living relative of what @thl is reaching for: 1970s machine-readable letterforms given humanist correction, presented in documentation that is itself an instrument panel. The datasheet is the useful artifact, not the typeface.

Take
The rule ladder — the datasheet uses at least three distinct rule weights to separate specimen from spec from footnote, which is §04 demonstrated at print resolution. Also the label weight: small-caps labels are set heavier than body, which is the 500 argument in §06. And the catalogue register (TX-02, FX-102, DX-102-11) is exactly the voice SYSTEM_ID: 0x8291 is aiming at.
Don't
Swap the typeface. IBM Plex Mono is already the right call and changing it is churn dressed as progress.
Accent economy · priority without colour

Places Journal — “Mission Control: A History of the Urban Dashboard”

placesjournal.org/article/mission-control-a-history-of-the-urban-dashboard/

A long-form history of the readout as a visual form, with substantial primary imagery of the rooms this aesthetic descends from.

Take
How real control rooms encode priority: almost entirely by position and size, with colour held in reserve for alarm. That is §03's argument with fifty years of operational evidence behind it — and it is why lime on a section-header bullet costs more than it looks like it costs.
Don't
The density. Those panels assume a trained operator who has memorised the layout; a documentation site does not get that assumption.
Glow · the physics of emission

CRT shader literature — diffusion, halation, phosphor persistence

Emulation General Wiki — Displays · Libretro CRT shader docs · Filthy Pants — “More CRT Shaders”

The most precise writing that exists on what emitted light actually does, because these authors had to model it numerically rather than describe it.

Take
The separation of diffusion (light spreading as it passes through the glass) from halation (a bright region illuminating adjacent dark areas by internal reflection), and the fact that both scale with emitted energy rather than with an element's geometry. That is the physical argument for “glow follows current, not geometry” in §05, and it specifies the shape: bright small-radius core, long low-alpha falloff, zero offset — the exact inverse of a drop shadow's large, offset, uniform spread.
Don't
Scanline overlays, barrel distortion, aperture-grille masks. @thl's scan line is a motion device tied to a reveal; a static scanline texture would be precisely the nostalgia the thesis disclaims.
Rule hierarchy · few sizes, large jumps

Josef Müller-Brockmann — Grid Systems in Graphic Design

Grid systems in graphic design · Draw Down

Take
Two things. “Few sizes, large jumps” — which @thl's closed scale already encodes and its rendered usage already violates, with 12 px carrying 69% of the reference page. And the practice of assigning rule weights meaning up front rather than picking a border per object, which is the discipline §04 is asking for.
Don't
The grid dogma. @thl's density is closer to a datasheet than a poster, and column-counting is not its problem.
The governing idea

Karl Gerstner — Designing Programmes

Designing Programmes (excerpt PDF) · Karl Gerstner

The theoretical parent of what this repo is actually doing — a rule set that generates its own artifacts rather than a set of finished layouts.

Take
Gerstner's test: “the maximum of conformity to a rule with the maximum of freedom.” Apply it to the accent. The constraint is one colour; the freedom is where it lands. Spending it on forty uniform table rows and eleven measuring bars forfeits the freedom while keeping the constraint — the worst of both.
Don't
Treat the token system as the design. Gerstner's programmes produced varied output; @thl's currently produces uniform output, which is a symptom of the same collapse.

10 — Arguing with the briefOne constraint I think is wrong

Everything above lives inside the seven constraints. This section does not, and is fenced off accordingly.

Constraint 7 — “Success has no colour. Lime is success.”

I think this is the weakest of the seven, and I think it is a direct cause of §03.

The reasoning given is sound as far as it goes: a separate green would both duplicate the accent and collide with it on a lime ground. Agreed — the answer is not to add a green. But the conclusion drawn from it is that success should be the same visual event as the brand mark, the focus ring, hover feedback, text selection and syntax highlighting. That is what makes NOMINAL read as decoration rather than as a reading: by the time the eye reaches it, lime has already fired eighteen times on that screen with six different meanings.

The system has an unused axis that resolves this without a new hue. Success can stay literally the accent and still be distinguishable, because colour is not the only channel:

“Success has no colour” survives intact — no green is added, lime is still success. What changes is that success stops being indistinguishable from decoration, using two levers (§04's weight ladder, §05's emission rule) that the system should adopt anyway. If the constraint is meant to say “add no green,” it is right and this is compatible with it. If it is meant to say “success needs no distinct treatment at all,” that is the part I would push back on.

For the record, the constraint I expected to argue with and did not: constraint 6, warning is orange. Amber genuinely does read as the accent on this ground. The problem with --color-warning is its lightness, not its hue, and §03 proposes a fix that keeps it orange.
