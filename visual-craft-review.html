The thesis is not yet delivered, and the reason is measurable rather than aesthetic. This system says borders are structural. In the rendered artefact, borders carry no structure at all — every line on the page is 1px at 1.52:1 against the ground, which is below the threshold at which a hairline survives a dim display, a squint, or an arm's length. Blur the pages and every border disappears. What survives is lime.

That is the whole diagnosis in one sentence: lime is over-used not because it appears too often, but because it is the only element in the system with enough contrast to do a job. Every job therefore falls to it — heading, label, bracket, badge, status, bullet, ruler, hover, selection. The accent has become the texture because nothing else has any.

The good news is that this is a system whose entire visual language is line, and it is currently spending exactly one line weight and one line value. There is a large, on-thesis, constraint-respecting move available — a real weight hierarchy — that costs no colour, no radius, no gradient, and no softness. And the strongest optical lever on near-black, emissive bloom, is already built correctly inside this codebase and confined to a single component that shows it for one second.

01_Value_Structure

The brief asks whether surface being darker than ground is intentional depth inversion, flatness, or a mistake. Measured, it is none of the three — it is a null.

So the advertised four-step ramp is really two values: a ground at roughly #0C0C0C and a line at roughly #363636, with a single 1.52:1 step between them. --color-surface and --color-ambient are not steps; they are rounding errors that have been given names and semantics. Panels do not sink, because 1.03:1 cannot sink. They simply are not there.

The squint test

This is the empirical core of the review. A system that stakes its identity on structural line has produced a value structure in which line contributes zero. Remove lime from these plates and there is no visual organisation left — no hierarchy, no grouping, no figure, no ground.

Does a line-based system need a value ramp at all?

No — and it should stop pretending to have one. Elevation is the wrong metaphor here: elevation implies light falling on stacked planes, which is the vocabulary of soft shadow and gradient that this system exists to reject. The right metaphor is the drawing sheet, where hierarchy comes from line grade, not from the paper changing colour.

The prescription is therefore not "add more greys." It is: collapse surface into ground, admit the system has one ground, and move the entire hierarchy budget into line weight (§03) and emissive light (§04). One honest ground plus a real weight ladder will read at every distance. Four grounds separated by 1.03:1 read at none.

One exception worth keeping. ImageFrame sets imageOpacity: 0.7 plus grayscale(100%) over surface — the only place in the system where imagery gets a value policy, and it works. It knocks photographs down toward the ground instead of letting them blow past it. Every other value decision in the system should be that deliberate; note that even so, the sky in the hero photo remains the brightest object on /demo, brighter than lime and brighter than white type.

On /demo, lime is the second most common text colour on the page — 17 runs against 9 for the body colour and 5 for white. On /design-system it appears 70 times, more often than white. In one screen it is doing all of this: a nav item, a headline word, three image badges, six numeric readouts, four NOMINAL statuses, a footer status, thirty-two bracket segments and nine fills.

The optical fact that explains everything. Lime #DFFF00 measures 16.83:1 against the ground. White measures 19.17:1. Lime against white is therefore 1.14:1 — they are the same value. Lime is not brighter than the primary ink; it is a hue swap at identical luminance. That is why the squint plates show the headline as two equally bright bars. An accent that matches your ink's value cannot create hierarchy — it can only label. And labelling is precisely what it has drifted into doing.

Where removing lime would make the remaining lime louder

  1. The spacing scale. Eleven solid lime bars used as a ruler. This is the single densest concentration of accent in the system and it carries no signal whatsoever — it measures millimetres. It makes §03 the loudest band on the reference page, louder than the colour section.
  2. Every table's first column. The token reference sets ~20 consecutive rows of lime property names; the type scale does the same. Lime has become a bullet glyph.
  3. BentoCard labels. Six cards, six lime micro-labels. A card title is not a signal; it is a title.
  4. Heading 4. The smallest heading is the only one that gets the loudest colour, which inverts the type hierarchy it belongs to.
  5. Inline emphasis in body copy. The landing page runs two emphasis tiers inside one paragraph — white and lime — five times across three paragraphs. At the 36-character mobile measure this reads as highlighter marker, not signal.

What lime should be reserved for: live state and nothing else. NOMINAL, RENDERING, IMG_SRC_LOADED, the scan line, focus, selection, and the current item in a set. Everything currently lime that is static — labels, table keys, card titles, rulers, headings — should drop to ink or ink-subtle. On a screen where lime marks only what is live, three lime events per viewport will read louder than seventy do today.

03_Line_Weight

Every distinct border specification actually rendered, with counts:

There is no weight hierarchy. There is one hairline, plus a second weight (2px) that is reserved entirely for a decorative motif and never used to mark importance. The four grey line treatments — #333, #3A3A3A, #3A3A3A at 50%, and their 2px variants — span 1.11:1 between the two named greys. They are one colour wearing four names.

The corner-bracket paradox

Look closely at the idle plate and the brackets are visible — but not because of colour. They are visible because they are 2px against a 1px border. The weight does one hundred per cent of the work; the colour change does none, and then muddies the token story by introducing a second grey to justify itself.

The system has already proved its own answer. In the one place it reaches for a second line weight, weight alone carries the distinction. That is the lever. Everything §01 said the value ramp cannot do, line grade can.

Two further consequences worth naming. First, ImageFrame and BentoCard share the 12px/2px bracket geometry and a comment claiming "the motif is one language" — but ImageFrame's brackets are permanently lime while BentoCard's are permanently invisible. Same shape, opposite loudness. Second, on BentoCard hover the fill goes darker (#0F0F0F → #0A0A0A) while the border goes maximally bright — the two halves of the hover state move in opposite directions. At 1.03:1 the fill change is imperceptible, so the contradiction costs nothing today; it will cost something the moment the ramp is fixed.

Proposed: three grades, one grey

ISO 128-2 gives technical drawing a line-width series stepped by √2 (0.18 / 0.25 / 0.35 / 0.5 / 0.7 / 1.0 / 1.4 / 2.0 mm), with extra-wide : wide : narrow held at 4 : 2 : 1 and a hard floor of 2:1 between thick and thin. Screen pixels are coarser than that, but the discipline transfers exactly: pick three grades, keep them at a 1 : 2 : 4 ratio, and give each one a meaning.

Proposed grade ladder — one colour, three weights, three jobs
Hairline · 1px
Structural · 2px
Emphatic · 4px
GradeMeaningApplied to
Hairline · 1pxSubdivision inside a thingTable rules, list separators, the grid overlay, inner dividers
Structural · 2pxThe edge of a thingCard, panel, frame, input, button — anything that is an object
Emphatic · 4pxThe edge of a region, or the active objectSection boundaries, the page's major divisions, focus, the selected card

Three moves follow immediately. Delete --color-ambient as a line value and let the grades carry the distinction it was invented for — a 1px rule and a 2px edge are already unambiguously different jobs, so they do not also need to be different greys. Raise the structural grade's value while you are there: at 2px, a line at 1.52:1 becomes legible; at 1px it never was. And give the corner brackets the emphatic grade, so the motif finally reads as a mark rather than a thickening of the border.

04_Glow_and_Bloom

Verified by source search: --shadow-lime-glow-lg is defined at theme.css:110, printed in the token reference table, and applied nowhere in the codebase. Its sibling --shadow-glow is applied exactly twice, both in image-frame.module.css (lines 27 and 45). Across the entire product surface, emissive light appears on one component, during one transient state. The audit found six lime shadows on a 9,005-pixel-tall reference page and zero on /demo.

Is emissive light the same thing as a drop shadow?

No — and the distinction is not a loophole, it is the substance of the thesis. A drop shadow is a claim about geometry: it asserts that an object floats above a plane, that there is a light source somewhere off-screen, and that the interface is a stack of physical cards. That is precisely the vocabulary of depth and atmosphere this system rejects.

Bloom is a claim about emission: it asserts that a thing is on. It is not borrowed from paper and light; it is borrowed from phosphor, LED and neon — from instruments that actually emit. On a near-black ground it is the single strongest available lever, because it is the only effect that can make one element brighter than white without changing its colour or its size. It is functional, not atmospheric. It belongs here.

This system has already written the correct bloom and then hidden it. The scan-line utility at theme.css:133–168 stacks four shadow layers at 8 / 20 / 40 / 80px with opacities 0.6 / 0.4 / 0.2 / 0.1, plus a 40px gradient tail. That is a textbook phosphor falloff — and it is the most optically sophisticated object in the system. It renders on a 2px line, inside ImageFrame, for about one second, and then never again.

Compare the third box with the first: a 1px lime edge on near-black already appears to bloom — the eye does the work. Glow therefore should not be applied to make lime visible; lime is already at 16.83:1. It should be applied to say this is live, and only there. Concretely:

Where it belongs

  • The active element — focus, the hovered card, the selected item. One at a time, per screen.
  • Genuine live state — RENDERING, an in-flight readout, a value that is changing.
  • Growing and scanning edges — where it already lives. Promote the four-layer scan-line recipe into a token so the weaker 15px version in image-frame.module.css:45 stops being a second, dimmer scan line.
  • Inward, not outward — an inset bloom (fourth box) reads as a lit chamber rather than a floating object, which suits a system with no elevation.

Where it cheapens

  • On anything static. A glowing label is a neon sign; a glowing status is an instrument.
  • On more than one element at a time. Bloom's whole value is that it is the loudest thing available. Two of them and it is wallpaper.
  • On type. Glow on lime text at 10px destroys the counters and the tracking work; if text must glow, use filter: drop-shadow() at a tight radius, never a stacked halo.
  • Behind photography. The hero image already out-values everything; adding bloom there fights a battle the photo has won.

The -lg variant should either be wired to the "active object" role or deleted. A token that is documented in a public reference table and has never rendered is a promise the system is not keeping.

05_Typography

Space Grotesk for display, IBM Plex Mono for body, is an unusual and genuinely strong pairing — a geometric grotesque that behaves like signage against a text face that behaves like a readout. The pairing is the best decision in the system. Its execution is spending about a third of what it bought.

Weight: five loaded, two used

layout.tsx:7,13 loads weights 300, 400, 500, 600 and 700 for both families — ten faces. A source-wide search for weight utilities returns font-bold ×25 and nothing else: zero uses of font-light, font-medium, font-semibold or font-normal. The rendered audit agrees — across ~300 text elements on the reference page, only three combinations exist: mono 400, mono 700, Space Grotesk 700.

With only 400 and 700 available, every act of emphasis has to be made with colour — which is a large part of why lime is doing seventy jobs. A 500 or 600 step is the cheapest de-liming move in the system: it gives labels and card titles a way to step forward without leaving the ink palette. This is exactly the move Otl Aicher made for ERCO, where hierarchy is carried by four decreasing weights of a single typeface rather than by colour.

The smoothing tax

The system's type is thinner than it needs to be for three compounding reasons: no mid weights, very small sizes, and global grayscale smoothing. Any one is survivable. All three together is why labels read as faint rather than precise. Scoping the rule to high-DPI displays, or dropping it, returns weight for free.

One component, two typefaces

base.css:31–38 assigns --font-sans to h1–h6. SectionHeader sets no family of its own and defaults to as="h2". The result: a purely semantic choice silently changes the typeface. Nine section headers on the reference page render in Space Grotesk; elsewhere the same component renders in IBM Plex Mono. The audit found both Space Grotesk 700 12px and IBM Plex Mono 700 12px co-existing at the same size and weight, doing the same job.

The two faces disagree about zero

This matters because of the finding above. Every section header is numbered — 01_TYPOGRAPHY, 03_SPACING_SCALE, 09_BENTO_CARD — and every one of them renders in the face that cannot distinguish 0 from O, while every hex readout, coefficient and badge in the system renders in the face that can. Same root cause as the typeface split; same one-line fix.

Scale, measure and the mobile headline

  • Two steps of the "closed" scale do not render. On the reference page text-2xl and text-6xl compute to 16px/24px instead of 24px and 60px — verified both by computed style and by injecting a bare element, so the utilities are genuinely absent from the emitted CSS rather than mis-marked up. The tokens themselves resolve correctly. In the specimen table, the 6xl row's sample is smaller than the sm row's.
  • Measure is well controlled on desktop — 47 to 71 characters across all body paragraphs, comfortably inside Bringhurst's 45–75. Two exceptions: / at 375px falls to ≈36 characters because the body stays at 16px while the column halves, and BentoCard prose sits at 38. For a fixed-advance face, size is the lever that must move when the column narrows.
  • The mobile headline loses its idea. At 1440 the /demo hero is a three-line stack with the lime word isolated on its own line — the composition is the accent's isolation. At 375 it breaks to ARCHITECTING FULL- / STACK EQUILIBRIUM: a dangling hyphen, and the accent no longer alone. The -0.05em tracking that reads as engineered at 60px also crowds badly at the smaller size, where ILIB in EQUILIBRIUM collapses into a single mass.
  • Tracking discipline is otherwise good. tracking-label at 0.2em is applied consistently — 23 instances at 12px and 22 at 10px, with only two orphan values on the whole page. This is the most disciplined part of the type system and it should be the model for the rest.

06_Density_Texture_Edge

The density is not the density it claims

The reference page is a 12px page — 184 elements at 12px, 52 at 10px, and almost nothing between 12px and 30px. That reads as instrument density. But the vertical rhythm is set at magazine spacing: roughly 90–100px between a section rule and its content, and section gaps far larger than anything the horizontal grid implies. The result is dense type floating in loose space — a readout printed on a poster.

Pick one. If the thesis is instrument, tighten the vertical to the same 4px grid the buttons already land on, and let the density be real. The type is already there; only the air disagrees.

The empty-container problem

Twelve BentoCards on /demo occupy roughly 750 vertical pixels each containing a single 10px hex label centred in near-black. Six more on the reference page do the same. With no border value, no fill difference and no internal structure, these are not objects — they are absences with labels.

This is where the grid overlay wants to live. It is currently built, sits behind ImageFrame, and is destroyed the moment an image loads. Moved into BentoCard at the hairline grade it would give an empty container texture, scale and a reason to exist.

Edge

Zero radius is honoured everywhere — no violations found. But "hard edge" is currently a statement about corner geometry only, when it should also be a statement about contrast at the boundary. A corner can be perfectly square and still have no edge, which is what 1.52:1 at 1px produces. The zero-radius rule is being kept; the harder claim underneath it is not.

Two smaller edge inconsistencies worth a pass: the ImageFrame badge fills with canvas (#0F0F0F) while the frame it sits inside fills with surface (#0A0A0A), so the chip is lighter than its container while every other panel is darker than its page; and the outline button, the tag button and the spec containers that hold them all render as the same 1px box at the same value — a component, a different component, and their wrapper, visually identical.

07_References_Worth_Stealing_From

Line grade
ISO 128-2 — Basic line conventions for technical product documentation

The line-width series is stepped by √2 (0.18 / 0.25 / 0.35 / 0.5 / 0.7 / 1.0 / 1.4 / 2.0 mm) — the same ratio that governs A-series paper, so a drawing reduced one size keeps its hierarchy intact. Extra-wide : wide : narrow is held at 4 : 2 : 1, with a hard floor of 2:1 between thick and thin.

Stealthe ratio discipline and the idea that each grade carries a fixed meaning across every drawing. This is the whole of §03, already standardised and battle-tested for a century.

SourceISO 128 · ISO 128-2:2022

Weight as hierarchy
Otl Aicher — ERCO identity (1976), and the Munich 1972 pictogram system

Aicher built the ERCO identity on Univers in four decreasing weights, and the Munich pictograms on a strict modular grid with consistent line weights throughout. Hierarchy is carried by weight and grid, never by colour.

Stealthe discipline of making an entire identity out of one typeface's weight range. This is the direct answer to a system that loaded five weights and used two, and it de-limes the interface without touching the palette.

SourceERCO (1976) — FGD1 The Archive · Otl Aicher, Ulm & Munich 1972

Instrument symbology
MIL-STD-1787 — Aircraft Display Symbology

The standard governing head-up display symbology: symbol geometry, fonts, recommended dimensions and mechanisation for the primary flight reference. The direct ancestor of the corner-bracket-and-reticle vocabulary this system uses, but with something it currently lacks — a strict rule about which marks are permitted to be present at once, because on a HUD every extra mark costs attention that is needed elsewhere.

Stealthe discipline of a fixed symbol budget per screen. It is the doctrinal version of §02's argument: brackets, reticles and status marks earn their place by being rare.

SourceMIL-STD-1787A · MIL-HDBK-87213 (electro-optical display design handbook)

Dark as the canonical surface
Vercel — Geist

The closest living relative: dark treated as the canonical surface rather than an alternate theme, restrained typography, monospaced numerals for technical labels, aggressive negative tracking at display sizes, and a deep neutral ramp. Notably it is almost entirely monochrome — no accent at all, with hierarchy carried by contrast, and it uses shadow-as-border to get a 1px edge without box-model cost.

Stealthe many-step neutral ramp — the thing §01 found missing — and study how far a dark system can get on contrast alone before reaching for an accent. Do not steal the monochrome: lime is this system's identity. Steal the restraint that surrounds it.

SourceGeist breakdown · The Vercel aesthetic

Emission, not elevation
CRT phosphor optics — fluorescence and persistence

Phosphor bloom is the physical behaviour this system's scan line is already imitating: light bleeding outward from an excited point, with a falloff that continues briefly after excitation stops. It is achieved with stacked shadow layers of increasing blur and decreasing opacity — bright colour outward reads as emission, dark inward reads as depth. filter: drop-shadow() traces alpha and suits type and non-rectangular marks; box-shadow suits boxes.

Stealthe falloff curve, which theme.css:133–168 has already got right, and promote it out of one component. Keep the reduced-motion guard that is already there.

SourceCRT emulation — the full signal chain · Layered-shadow glow technique

Measure and smoothing
Bringhurst's 45–75, WCAG 1.4.8, and the antialiasing argument

45–75 characters is the accepted band for a single column of body text, with WCAG AAA capping at 80. Monospace runs about 0.60 em per character, which makes the arithmetic exact and makes size the only lever when a column narrows. Separately, the long-standing argument against setting -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased globally: it thins small type, and the targeted form is to scope it to high-DPI displays or to a dark-mode query.

Stealthe 45-character floor as a hard mobile constraint, and the scoped smoothing rule.

SourceGoogle Fonts — Understanding measure · modern-normalize #19 · Zach Leatherman on font smoothing

Numerals
IBM Plex Mono — zero alternates and the webfont subset

Plex Mono ships a slashed zero (ss03, aliased to the zero feature) and a dotted variant (ss04), but the stylistic alternates are not included in the Latin subset of the webfont build — which is what next/font/google with subsets: ['latin'] loads. Tested here: neither feature changes the glyph; the default dotted zero is what renders, and it is already unambiguous.

Stealnothing — the mono side is fine. The action is on the display face, which needs either a zero that is not its capital O, or simply to stop rendering numbered labels.

SourceIBM/plex #162 — missing OpenType features in webfonts · Plex Mono feature guide

08_Prescriptions

Ranked by visual return per unit of change. Every one of these respects dark-only, zero-radius, no-gradient, the 4.5:1 floor, orange-not-amber, and success-is-lime.

RX_01Build the three-grade line systemHighest return

Hairline 1px / structural 2px / emphatic 4px, one grey, each grade with a fixed meaning. Raise the structural value so a 2px edge clears the ground properly. This is the move that makes the squint plates show structure instead of lime, and it is the only prescription here that changes the system's whole character.

RX_02Take lime off everything staticHighest return

Spacing bars, table key columns, card labels, Heading 4, inline body emphasis. Lime marks live state and current selection only. Target roughly three lime events per viewport instead of seventeen to seventy.

RX_03Add one mid weight and use it for emphasisHigh · low cost

500 or 600 is already downloaded. It gives labels and titles a way to step forward inside the ink palette, which is what makes RX_02 survivable. Pair with scoping or dropping the global antialiased rule to get the stroke back.

RX_04Collapse the ramp to one groundHigh · low cost

Fold surface into ground and stop ambient from being a line value. Two tokens whose only measurable property is that they are not distinguishable are costing more in confusion than they return in appearance.

RX_05Promote bloom out of ImageFrameMedium-high

Tokenise the four-layer scan-line falloff, retire the weaker duplicate at image-frame.module.css:45, and give the -lg variant a job — the active object, one per screen — or delete it.

RX_06Move the grid overlay into the empty containersMedium-high

It is already built and currently visible for one second per image before being hidden behind a photograph. At the hairline grade inside BentoCard it solves the largest remaining hole in the layout — thousands of vertical pixels of textureless black.

RX_07Give SectionHeader an explicit typefaceMedium · trivial

One class fixes two findings: the component stops changing face with its heading level, and numbered labels stop rendering in the face whose zero is its capital O.

RX_08Fix the fixed-advance mobile measureMedium

Step the body down at narrow widths so / clears the 45-character floor instead of sitting at 36, and set the /demo headline so the accent word keeps its own line. Consider a display step between 36px and 60px — this would be a deliberate addition to the closed scale, not an arbitrary value.

RX_09Desaturate disabled instead of fading itLow · trivial

disabled:opacity-40 on lime manufactures #626F09, a colour in no token. Render disabled in ink-subtle so the palette stays closed.

RX_10Repair the two missing scale stepsLow · trivial

text-2xl and text-6xl render at 16px because the class strings are built from generated data and never appear literally in scanned source. A safelist or a literal map restores them. Worth doing simply because the specimen table currently disproves its own caption.

RX_11Let the landing page use the systemLow effort · large impression

/ renders three lime text runs, one 8×8 square, eight identical hairlines and no brackets, no grid, no scan line, no badge. The front door is the one page that demonstrates none of the vocabulary it describes in prose. Nothing here needs inventing — only using.

09_Argued_Exception

One constraint I think is genuinely worth revisiting. Flagged here rather than smuggled into the body, as instructed.

"No soft shadows" should be restated as "no shadows that imply elevation"

As written, the rule bans a technique. What it means to ban is a claim — the claim that the interface is a stack of physical planes lit from off-screen. Emissive bloom makes the opposite claim: that a thing is powered. It is the native optics of every instrument this system references — phosphor, LED, neon, HUD.

The system already agrees with me in practice and does not know it. theme.css:133–168 contains a linear-gradient and a four-layer stacked halo, inside a system whose landing page copy begins "No soft gradients." That is not a violation; it is the correct instinct arriving without permission. Restating the rule would let bloom be designed deliberately, budgeted per screen, and tokenised — rather than smuggled into one CSS module and left there.

Everything else — dark-only, zero radius, the 4.5:1 floor, orange over amber, lime as success — I would keep exactly as written. The orange-not-amber note in particular is the kind of reasoning most systems never write down and then relearn annually.
